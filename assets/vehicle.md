# Vehicle Troubleshooting Reference

## Engine will not crank
A single click or silence points to the battery, starter, or a bad ground. Check battery terminals for corrosion and tightness, then measure resting voltage: 12.6 V is a healthy lead-acid battery, below 12.0 V is discharged, below 11.8 V is likely failed. A jump start that runs and then dies after removal points to the alternator, not the battery.

## Engine cranks but will not start
Separate fuel from spark. If it fires briefly on starting fluid, suspect fuel delivery: check the fuel pump fuse and relay, listen for the pump prime for two seconds at key-on, and check for a clogged filter. If it does not fire at all, check for spark at a pulled coil lead. A security light staying on indicates an immobilizer fault.

## Overheating
Pull over, shut off the engine, and let it cool before opening the pressure cap. Low coolant, a failed thermostat, a broken water pump belt, or a seized fan are the common causes. A heater that blows cold while the engine overheats suggests low coolant. Never add cold water to an overheated engine; thermal shock can crack the head.

## Battery and charging
A healthy alternator holds 13.8 to 14.6 V across the battery at idle with loads on. Below 13.2 V means the alternator is not charging. A battery that reads 12.6 V but cannot crank is likely sulfated or has a bad cell; load testing is the reliable check.

## Brakes
Soft or spongy pedal: air or moisture in the hydraulic system, or a failing master cylinder. Pedal that sinks slowly while held: internal leak. Grinding under braking: worn pads contacting the rotor; stop driving. Pull to one side: seized caliper or collapsed hose. Brake fluid absorbs water and should be replaced every two to three years.

## Tire failure
A blowout at speed: hold the wheel firmly, ease off the accelerator, do not brake hard, and steer straight until speed drops below 40 km/h. Change to the spare on level ground with the parking brake set and wheels chocked. Torque lug nuts in a star pattern.

## Electrical faults
Check fuses first, then grounds. Intermittent faults that appear in wet weather usually trace to a corroded connector or a chafed harness. A parasitic drain above 50 mA at rest will flatten a battery overnight; isolate it by pulling fuses one at a time and watching the meter.

## Diesel specifics
A diesel that cranks but will not fire may have air in the fuel system after running dry; prime it before cranking to protect the injection pump. White smoke at start indicates unburned fuel from cold cylinders or a failed glow plug. Loss of power under load with black smoke points to a restricted air filter or boost leak.
