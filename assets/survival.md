# Field Survival Reference

## Priority of survival
Survival priorities in order: shelter, water, fire, signal, food. Exposure kills faster than thirst, and thirst faster than hunger. In cold and wet conditions, build shelter and get dry before searching for water. In desert heat, move at night, rest in shade during the day, and ration sweat rather than water.

## Water procurement
Assume 3 liters per person per day in temperate conditions, 5 to 6 liters in heat or during hard work. Never drink untreated surface water without assessing the risk. Boiling for one minute at sea level (three minutes above 2000 m) kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa and is the most reliable field method. Chemical treatment with chlorine dioxide requires 4 hours contact time and is ineffective against Cryptosporidium. Filter plus boil when the source is suspect.

Solar disinfection (SODIS) needs 6 hours of direct sun in clear PET bottles, and works poorly in cloudy water. Dew collection and transpiration bags yield small amounts, typically under a liter per day, and should be treated as supplements only.

## Fire craft
Three ingredients: tinder, kindling, fuel. Dry inner bark, charred cloth, and fine shavings catch a spark; resin-rich pine knots sustain it. Build a platform on wet ground. In wind, shield the ignition point with your body or a rock wall. A ferrocerium rod throws sparks hotter than a lighter flint and works when wet.

Signal fires should be built in threes in a triangle or line, on high ground, with green foliage ready to make white smoke by day.

## Shelter
A debris hut traps body heat: a ridgepole, ribs, and 60 cm of dry leaf litter, with the entrance plugged with a door bundle. Insulate from the ground — contact with cold soil conducts heat away faster than cold air. In snow, dig a quinzhee and mark the roof with a ski pole so it can be found.

## Navigation without instruments
The sun rises in the east and sets in the west; at local noon it is due south in the northern hemisphere and due north in the southern. Cast a shadow stick: mark the tip of the shadow 15 minutes apart, and the line between marks runs west to east. At night, Polaris sits within one degree of true north; find it from the pointer stars of Ursa Major.

## Signaling
Three of anything is the universal distress signal: three fires, three whistle blasts, three flashes. A signal mirror can be seen 30 km away in clear air. Ground-to-air symbols: V means need assistance, X means need medical assistance, arrow means traveling in this direction.

## Cold injury
Hypothermia: uncontrolled shivering, clumsiness, slurred speech, then paradoxical undressing. Treat with dry clothing, insulation, and warm sweet drinks if conscious; do not rub limbs or give alcohol. Frostbite: numb, waxy, pale skin. Rewarm in water at 37 to 39 degrees Celsius, never over an open flame, and never rewarm if refreezing is possible.
