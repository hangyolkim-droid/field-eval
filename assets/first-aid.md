# Field First Aid

## Massive bleeding
Apply direct pressure with the heel of the hand through a dressing. If bleeding continues, pack the wound tightly with gauze or clean cloth, including deep into the wound cavity, and hold pressure for at least three minutes without peeking. A tourniquet is indicated for life-threatening limb bleeding that pressure cannot control: place it 5 to 7 cm above the wound, never over a joint, tighten until bleeding stops, and record the time. Do not loosen a tourniquet once placed; evacuate.

## CPR
Adult: 30 chest compressions at 100 to 120 per minute, 5 to 6 cm deep, then 2 rescue breaths. Continue until help arrives or the patient breathes. Hands-only CPR is acceptable if unwilling or unable to give breaths. Compressions restart circulation; they do not restart the heart. An AED should be applied as soon as available, and its prompts followed.

## Choking
Adult conscious: five back blows between the shoulder blades, then five abdominal thrusts, alternating. Infant: five back blows and five chest thrusts with two fingers, never abdominal thrusts. If the patient becomes unresponsive, begin CPR.

## Shock
Signs: pale or gray skin, rapid weak pulse, rapid shallow breathing, confusion, thirst. Lay the patient flat, raise the legs if no spinal or leg injury, keep warm, and do not give food or drink. Shock is a circulation failure, not a psychological state.

## Burns
Cool with running water for 20 minutes, not ice. Remove rings and tight items before swelling. Do not pop blisters. Cover with a clean non-adherent dressing. A burn is an emergency if it covers more than 10 percent of body surface, involves the face, hands, feet, or genitals, or is circumferential.

## Fractures and splinting
Immobilize the joint above and below the break. Check pulse, sensation, and movement beyond the injury before and after splinting. Pad the splint. Do not attempt to straighten an angulated fracture unless circulation is absent.

## Heat illness
Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea. Move to shade, loosen clothing, cool with wet cloths, and give water with salt if fully conscious. Heat stroke: hot dry skin or profuse sweating with confusion or collapse — a medical emergency. Cool aggressively with water and fanning, and evacuate.

## Anaphylaxis
Signs after exposure: hives, swelling of the face or throat, wheezing, hypotension. Epinephrine auto-injector into the outer thigh, hold 10 seconds, and repeat after 5 to 15 minutes if symptoms persist. Antihistamines do not replace epinephrine.

## Wound cleaning
Irrigate with clean water under pressure, ideally 500 ml or more for a dirty wound. Remove visible debris. Do not use hydrogen peroxide or alcohol in the wound bed; both damage tissue. Cover and monitor for redness, swelling, pus, or fever over the following days.
